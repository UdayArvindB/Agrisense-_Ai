# Title: Maize and Cereal Common Rust Management Manual
# Category: Crop Disease
# Source: International Cereal Pathology Network
# Year: 2026

## Disease Characteristics
Common rust of maize is caused by the obligate biotrophic fungus Puccinia sorghi. The fungus produces small, circular-to-elongate golden-brown to cinnamon-brown powdery pustules (uredinia) scattered across both upper and lower leaf surfaces.

## Environmental Drivers
Puccinia sorghi proliferates under moderate temperatures (16°C–25°C) combined with high relative humidity and dew durations exceeding 6 hours. High nitrogen fertilization without adequate potassium creates succulent leaf cuticles prone to fungal hyphal penetration.

## Control & Integrated Management
1. Hybrid selection: Planting certified rust-resistant hybrid varieties carrying specific single-gene (Rp genes) or multi-gene partial resistance.
2. Fungicide application threshold: Fungicides (such as strobilurins and triazole mixtures) are economically justified if pustules appear on leaves near or above the ear leaf prior to tasseling and weather forecasts predict sustained dew periods.
3. Early planting: Sow early in the planting calendar to allow crops to mature past vulnerable vegetative phases before mid-season airborne spore migrations arrive.
