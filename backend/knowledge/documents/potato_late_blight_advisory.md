# Title: Late Blight Alert & Emergency Foliar Advisory
# Category: Crop Disease
# Source: Regional Tuber & Solanaceae Pathology Council
# Year: 2026

## Etiology of Phytophthora Infestans
Late blight is caused by the oomycete water-mold Phytophthora infestans. It is an aggressive, fast-moving foliar and tuber disease capable of destroying entire crop plots within 7 to 10 days during cool, continuously damp weather (15°C–22°C with persistent moisture).

## Symptoms & Identification
- Rapidly expanding pale green to dark water-soaked lesions that turn purplish-brown and necrotic.
- Under humid conditions, a delicate white fuzzy downy mildew growth appears on the undersides of infected leaves along lesion borders.
- Dark brown water-soaked lesions develop on petioles and stems, leading to complete canopy collapse.

## Emergency Response & Management
1. Immediately remove and bury or destroy heavily infected plants to stop airborne sporangia clouds.
2. In commercial fields, apply systemic translaminar fungicides (e.g. Cymoxanil or Dimethomorph combined with a protectant) within 24 hours of first detection.
3. Hill up soil generously around potato ridges to prevent swimming zoospores from washing down into developing tubers during rain storms.
4. Clean and disinfect machinery and footwear between field sections to prevent mechanical transfer.
