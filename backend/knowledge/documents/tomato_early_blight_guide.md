# Title: Tomato Early Blight Management & Prevention Guide
# Category: Crop Disease
# Source: State Agricultural Research Station Bulletin
# Year: 2026

## Overview of Alternaria Solani
Early blight, caused by the fungal pathogen Alternaria solani, affects tomato and potato crops worldwide. The fungus produces characteristic concentric brown circular lesions on lower leaves, resembling target rings or a bullseye pattern. Yellow chlorotic margins typically surround the dark necrotic centers.

## Optimal Growth Conditions
The disease progresses rapidly when foliage remains wet for extended periods (exceeding 2 to 4 hours) under warm temperatures between 24°C and 29°C with high relative humidity (>80%). Spores are dispersed through wind currents and splashing water droplets from rainfall or overhead sprinkler irrigation.

## Recommended Cultural & Immediate Practices
1. Prune and destroy lower infected leaves located within 25-30 cm of the soil line. Do not compost infected leaves; bag and discard away from the field plot.
2. Cease overhead irrigation immediately. Switch exclusively to basal drip irrigation to keep upper canopy leaves dry.
3. Ensure adequate plant spacing (at least 60 cm between rows) to allow cross-canopy air circulation and accelerate drying of morning dew.

## Preventive and Bio-Fungicide Recommendations
Apply protective copper-based sprays (copper hydroxide or copper octanoate) or bio-fungicide formulations such as Bacillus subtilis or Trichoderma viride every 7 to 10 days during warm, humid weather. Organic straw mulching (5 to 8 cm depth) acts as a physical barrier preventing soil-borne fungal spores from splashing onto lower leaves.
